# A recipe is held by a Kitchen, not a person

Kamosu is multi-user, and this is the one part of it with no precedent to borrow: Hatchdoor is explicitly single-operator. A recipe is owned by a **Kitchen** — a named circle of People — and only that Kitchen may edit it. A **Person** cooks in one or more Kitchens, and a Person on their own is a Kitchen of one, so there is no solo mode sitting beside a shared one.

_(Amended by [ADR 0041](./0041-a-recipe-is-written-in-a-cookbook-and-seen-in-a-kitchen.md): a recipe belongs to a **Cookbook**, and only its **Co-authors** may change it; a Kitchen holds no recipes and decides only who sees them. The separation of seeing from changing, which this ADR is for, survives in two circles instead of one. The **Home Kitchen** is retired. So is the rule below that the last member may not leave a Kitchen, since a Kitchen holds nothing to strand, and leaving now takes your Cookbook out of it rather than leaving your recipes behind.)_

Everything else is a consequence of separating two acts that permission systems usually conflate. **Seeing** is governed by a recipe's **Visibility** — its Kitchen, Chosen People and Kitchens, Everyone on this instance, or Anyone with the link — and grants reading, cooking and recording an Attempt, never editing. _(Amended by [ADR 0026](./0026-a-recipe-is-seen-by-its-kitchen-or-by-anyone-with-the-link.md): the scale is reduced to its two ends — a recipe is seen by its Kitchen, or by anyone with its Share Link — and the term **Visibility** is retired. The separation of seeing from changing, which is what this ADR is for, is unaffected.)_ **Changing** something you can see is a **Copy**: a new Branch of the same Lineage, held by your Kitchen, indistinguishable from a share bundle arriving from another server.

An agent is never an account. A **Credential** always names a Person and comes either from a login or from an **Access Key** that Person minted.

## Why

- **Two people cooking from one recipe are one kitchen, not two correspondents.** [ADR 0004](./0004-one-lineage-many-branches.md) makes editing a shared recipe produce a Branch, and Kamosu never merges Branches. Applied to a couple, that would split the tartiflette permanently the first time one of them fixed it, and turn the divergence screen of a rare event into a nightly occurrence. Making the Kitchen the holder of a Branch means co-authoring is ordinary editing — one history, two names on it — while never-merge continues to hold everywhere else, unamended.
- **The editing circle is the only line that needs drawing.** Inside a Kitchen a recipe is one thing; outside it, every share is a Copy. That single sentence answers same-instance sharing, cross-instance sharing and import at once, because they are all the same act. No mechanism exists for "shared reference", so none can leak.
- **You can cook in more than one Kitchen.** A Branch still has exactly one holder, so multi-membership costs nothing structurally, and it makes leaving harmless: recipes stay where they were. Its one real cost — which Kitchen owns something you just wrote — is paid by a **Home Kitchen** default, and the question is only ever put to people in more than one.
- **Seeing must not imply editing, because seeing is what [ADR 0005](./0005-an-attempt-is-a-version-you-cooked-but-did-not-keep.md) already relies on.** Anyone who can see a recipe may record an Attempt against it; that is safe precisely because recording touches nothing the author wrote. Copy-on-change extends the same principle to the recipe itself.
- **Nothing here needs a permission matrix.** Two roles exist — Person and Operator — and Visibility has four values. On a home server run for a family, anything finer would be administered by nobody and understood by no one.

## Considered options

- **A Household you belong to exactly one of.** Rejected once it was clear a person may plausibly cook in their own kitchen, a parent's and a supper club's at the same time. The single-membership rule forced recipes to migrate on joining and orphaned people on leaving, and bought nothing.
- **No group at all — reciprocal per-person grants** ("let this Person see everything I own"). Rejected because it cannot express co-authoring: without a holder that outlives an individual, every edit to someone else's recipe is a Branch, and a couple's shared cookbook becomes a permanent fan of divergences.
- **A read-only tier below Copy.** Rejected as theatre. Nothing prevents a person retyping a recipe they can read, so a rule against copying would constrain only the honest.
- **A recipe held by a Kitchen** (chosen).

## Consequences

- **A Kitchen is named by its creator and nicknamed by its members.** The shared Name is what everyone sees and any member may change it; a Nickname is one Person's private relabelling, seen by nobody else and never travelling. Two Kitchens called "Home" stop being confusing without anyone negotiating.
- **A share link is public and permanent, not a scoped grant.** One per recipe, no expiry, resharing expected — the honest description of a URL you texted to somebody. The page shows the recipe and its Translations and offers two things: the share bundle of [ADR 0004](./0004-one-lineage-many-branches.md), carrying the complete written history exactly as any other receiver gets it, and a PDF for the overwhelming majority who do not run Kamosu. Withdrawal is turning sharing off; turning it back on mints a new address, so a withdrawn link stays dead.
- **Attempts are carved out of visibility inheritance where a share link is concerned.** [ADR 0005](./0005-an-attempt-is-a-version-you-cooked-but-did-not-keep.md) has Attempts inherit a recipe's visibility, and they never leave the instance in any case; stated positively, a Share Link publishes the recipe and its history and never a cooking diary. Written history travels; *"burned it, two stars, the kids refused"* does not.
- **There is no signup.** The first run mints the Operator; every account after that begins with a one-use Invite. Name and password, no email address — a home server cannot send mail, so a password reset is the Operator doing it, and demanding an address Kamosu can never write to would be decoration.
- **An agent borrows a Person's account rather than owning one.** Access Keys are named, individually revocable, never expiring and optionally read-only — the opt-in posture [ADR 0001](./0001-both-doors-generated-from-one-operation-catalogue.md) anticipated. They carry that Person's rights whole; there is no per-Kitchen or per-Operation narrowing to configure or misconfigure. **A Key cannot mint a Key** — that requires a login — or a read-only Key's first act could be to promote itself, and the flag would mean nothing. A dedicated bot needs no new concept: invite it as a Person.
- **The instance always knows which Access Key wrote a Version**, which is the input [#19](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/19) needs to decide what authorship is worth once Versions cross between servers.
- **Foods are instance-wide; Tags belong to a Kitchen.** A Food is a fact about the world and [the nutrition research](../research/nutrition-and-barcode-sources.md) found matching one is the expensive part, so it is paid once for the whole instance; only the Operator may merge two, that being the single destructive act reaching into other people's Kitchens. A Tag is how a Kitchen describes its own cookbook, and tags arriving with a recipe land in the receiving Kitchen's list. The obvious objection to instance-wide Foods — someone renames flour and my recipes change — is dissolved by [ADR 0002](./0002-the-written-ingredient-line-is-the-truth.md): a recipe always displays the Ingredient Line as written, and a Food's name surfaces only in shopping lists, nutrition and search.
- **Leaving destroys nothing.** Recipes stay with the Kitchen, Versions keep the name of whoever wrote them because history is append-only, and Attempts follow the Person, remaining legible to them even after they can no longer open the recipe. Nobody may leave a Kitchen they are the last member of: they must invite someone or delete it, or its recipes would be held by a circle no one can reach.
- **The Operator administers and does not read.** No view-as-user, no support mode, no screen anywhere onto another Person's recipes or Attempts. This is documented as a courtesy boundary rather than a security one, because the Operator holds the disk and can reset any password; a self-hosted app should say that plainly instead of implying a wall it cannot build. More than one Operator is allowed and the last cannot be demoted.
- **A Vault belongs to a Kitchen, amending [ADR 0003](./0003-the-database-is-the-truth-the-vault-is-a-published-copy.md)**, which defined it as per-person before Kitchens existed. Two members publishing the same recipes into two folders was never wanted. This also answers most of what "a shared vault" was waiting on: it is a Kitchen's Vault, and the people who may add to it are its members.
