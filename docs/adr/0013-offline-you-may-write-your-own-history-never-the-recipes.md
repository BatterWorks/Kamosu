# Offline, you may write your own history, never the recipe's

_(Amended by [ADR 0052](./0052-a-demo-refuses-what-a-stranger-could-misuse.md): a Demo copies nothing to a phone unasked and offers no install card ([#426](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/426)).)_

Kamosu works without a network, and the line between what it will and will not do is not drawn at convenience. **Everything on an Attempt's side of Promotion works offline; nothing on the recipe's side does.** Read any recipe, start cooking it, advance steps, tick ingredients, deviate, photograph it, finish, rate it, write a note — all with no server, syncing when one returns. Editing a recipe, promoting an **As Cooked** into a **Version**, or taking a **Copy** requires the server, and the interface says so rather than failing.

The boundary already had a name. [ADR 0005](./0005-an-attempt-is-a-version-you-cooked-but-did-not-keep.md) defines **Promotion** as "the point at which an Attempt's freedoms end and the recipe's rules begin" — written about a quite different problem, and describing this one exactly.

## Why

- **An offline edit queue is a merge, and Kamosu never merges.** Replaying an edit made on a phone onto a recipe changed meanwhile on the iPad is precisely the combining [ADR 0004](./0004-one-lineage-many-branches.md) spent a whole decision refusing, on the grounds that "less salt" plus "more butter" is a dish nobody cooked. Allowing offline recipe edits would have forced an exception into the one rule the model is built on, so that a typo fixed on a train could permanently fork your own recipe.
- **The permitted writes need no reconciling, because they are facts about one person on one afternoon.** [ADR 0010](./0010-a-cook-in-progress-is-an-unfinished-attempt.md) already settled that with two devices "the last one moved on is where the cook is". Last-one-wins is not a compromise for the offline case; it is what those things already meant. The offline behaviour was decided before this ticket existed.
- **Offline Attempts cannot conflict at all — this was found, not designed.** An Attempt pins to the Version cooked **by fingerprint** ([ADR 0005](./0005-an-attempt-is-a-version-you-cooked-but-did-not-keep.md)), and Versions are append-only and never deleted ([ADR 0004](./0004-one-lineage-many-branches.md)). Cook at your parents' house with no signal, rate it, come home to a recipe someone edited while you were out: the Version you cooked still exists, so your Attempt lands unchanged and correctly reports cooking the older one. The ugly case in most offline systems is already closed here by decisions made for other reasons.
- **The bar was Crouton, and Crouton's feel is achievable without Crouton's architecture.** What "works offline like Crouton" means in practice is that the app never goes blank and a cook never stops. Both are on the Attempt's side of Promotion. Genuine local-first — everything writable offline, conflicts becoming extra Branches — was rejected as a large amount of machinery bought for the case where two people edit one recipe in the same afternoon while one of them has no signal.

## Consequences

- **The whole library lives on the phone, photographs included.** Your Kitchens' recipes are cached in full, always; anything else you can see under [ADR 0007](./0007-a-recipe-is-held-by-a-kitchen-not-a-person.md) is cached once opened — *your cookbook is on your phone, the rest of the instance is on the server*. Nothing you own is ever mysteriously unavailable, and opening Kamosu never downloads a stranger's recipes.
- **The server must serve a Display Copy of a photograph, never the original.** This is a requirement on [#22](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/22), and it is what makes the above affordable. The real Crouton export was 110 MB, essentially all images ([#5](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/5)) — but those are camera originals at four thousand pixels, and a phone never displays more than about twelve hundred. Resized once on the server, the same 86 recipes are roughly 13 MB. Originals stay on the server for Export, PDF and Backup. Attempt photographs are not cached: they are a diary, rarely re-read, and they grow without bound.
- **The app draws from its own copy first and refreshes behind it**, so opening Kamosu is instant and never shows a spinner over content it already holds. The first fill waits for wifi by default, with a button to override.
- **HTTPS is a precondition, not a preference.** Service workers require a secure connection, and without a service worker there is no offline Kamosu on any platform — plus no Wake Lock and no storage that survives. This is a hard requirement on [#27](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/27), where it was not previously known.
- **All of it depends on Kamosu being installed to the home screen.** On iOS a site used in a Safari tab has its storage wiped after seven days unused, and tab storage and installed-app storage are entirely separate, so someone who uses Kamosu in a tab for a month and then installs it starts from an empty phone. Kamosu cannot trigger installation on iOS — Apple offers no such button — so it explains, with device-appropriate instructions, and says plainly in settings that offline recipes need it installed. Refusing to work uninstalled was rejected: it would put a wall in front of the first thing anyone does with a Share Link.
- **A shopping list is neither an Attempt nor a recipe, and this rule does not cover it.** A list shared inside a Kitchen, ticked by two people in two shops with bad signal, is concurrent editing of shared state. [#25](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/25) owns it and must decide its semantics knowing that offline ticking is expected.
- **The Share Link page is rendered by the Rust server as plain HTML**, not by the app. Its reader is a stranger with no account arriving from a message, and a page drawn in the browser has nothing for a messaging app to read, so a shared recipe arrives as a naked URL instead of a card with its photograph. No amount of frontend work fixes that. It does not dent **Parity**: the page *consumes* an Operation, it is not one. The server already renders recipes to PDF ([#24](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/24)) and will render them to Markdown for a Vault ([ADR 0003](./0003-the-database-is-the-truth-the-vault-is-a-published-copy.md)); HTML is a third form of the same job.
- **The interface ships in the app, in all three languages.** Fetching translations would put a network round-trip in front of the first screen and break offline. All of fr/en/es are precached — tens of kilobytes — so switching language offline works as a side effect. Reading Language belongs to a **Person** and therefore to the server, so the device remembers the last known one and falls back to the phone's own language on a fresh install; it never shows one language and swaps to another.
