# An Ingredient Line has no name of its own

_(Amended by [ADR 0050](./0050-a-translations-line-takes-the-food-of-the-line-in-its-place.md): a Translation's Ingredient Line is matched with its source line by place, for its Food alone, since Pairing cannot cross a Language. Pairing itself is unchanged.)_

Nothing is stapled to a line of a recipe. An Ingredient Line and a Step carry no id, no key and no serial number — not in the database, not in the share bundle, not in the Vault's `.kamosu/` sidecar. When Kamosu needs to know that your `1 cup potato starch (or corn starch)` and Marc's `¾ cup potato starch` are the same line changed rather than one line removed and another added, it works it out by reading, against the **Branch Point** — the last Version the two Branches shared.

That act has a name: a **Pairing**. Two lines are paired, or one is left unpaired and becomes a **Ghost**.

Where the reading is uncertain, Kamosu declines to pair and shows both lines. It never labels a Pairing as a guess.

## Why

- **A stored id cannot both travel and stay honest.** A Version is named by a fingerprint of its content ([ADR 0004](./0004-one-lineage-many-branches.md)). Put line ids inside that content and two people who independently add `1 tsp salt` to the same recipe produce two different Versions of a textually identical recipe — a divergence manufactured out of nothing, in a system whose whole point is that identical states converge without communicating. Leave line ids outside the content, as a Version's name is left outside it, and two holders of "the same Version" hold different ids, so any comparison between their descendants must fall back on reading the words anyway. Either branch of that fork ends at Pairing. Once Pairing exists and works, the ids earn nothing.
- **Ids die exactly when a person is being ordinary.** Select-all-delete-retype is how a line gets fixed on a phone, and it kills the id for text that barely changed. Pasting a better ingredient list over the old one kills every id at once, so a recipe where three lines changed reports itself wholly deleted and wholly rewritten. Pairing survives both, because the words survive both.
- **The Branch Point makes the guessing surface small.** Kamosu is never matching two lists blind. Any line neither side touched is identical on both sides and needs no Pairing at all; only the lines somebody actually edited need reading, and in a fifteen-line list that is typically one to three. This is the same three-way footing git stands on, over text that is shorter and far less repetitive than code.
- **It is the grain of every decision already made.** A Version is a complete state, not a delta ([ADR 0004](./0004-one-lineage-many-branches.md)). An As Cooked is a complete state, not a record of deviations ([ADR 0005](./0005-an-attempt-is-a-version-you-cooked-but-did-not-keep.md)). An Ingredient is a written line, not a bundle of fields ([ADR 0002](./0002-the-written-ingredient-line-is-the-truth.md)). A hidden permanent name on a line would be the one thing Kamosu stores that a cook never wrote and can never see.
- **It costs the bundle and the sidecar nothing**, because there is nothing to add. A stored-shape question under [ADR 0009](./0009-the-v1-cut-line-is-drawn-at-shape-not-at-features.md) turns out to require no shape.

## Considered options

- **A minted id per line**, carried through every edit and travelling in the bundle. The obvious answer and the one the [#18](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/18) prototype faked, where every line carried a hand-written key. Rejected on the two grounds above: it cannot sit inside the fingerprint without manufacturing divergence, cannot sit outside it without needing Pairing as a fallback, and it dies on retyping and paste-over — the two most ordinary ways a line gets changed.
- **An id derived from the line's own text**, so identical text is identical id. Rejected as self-defeating: editing the text changes the id, which is the precise thing the identity was for.
- **Position in the list.** Rejected — it breaks the moment anything is inserted, which is most edits.
- **An id that lives on this instance and never travels.** Rejected: the requirement is a cross-instance one. Two people editing the same line on two servers have to reach the same answer with nothing to ask each other.
- **Pairing, read against the Branch Point** (chosen).

## Consequences

- **When Kamosu is unsure, it shows both lines rather than claiming a connection.** Given your `200 g farine` and Marc's `200 g sucre` with nothing else nearby to match, it does not pair them: your line stands, his shows as a Ghost, and no offer appears to write one over the other. The two failure modes are not symmetric. An unpaired line costs a few seconds of extra reading with every word of both recipes still on the page; a bad Pairing asserts that two unrelated ingredients are one ingredient and puts a *take his* button underneath it.
- **A Pairing is never labelled as a guess.** No "probably the same line" hedge. The readability comes from the design rather than a disclaimer: whichever way it falls, both texts are on screen in full, so a wrong Pairing is visible as a wrong Pairing and a cook can ignore it. A confidence badge on some markings and not others would add a decision to every line while giving the cook nothing to decide with — the actual text is better evidence than a label about the text.
- **A fully rewritten Step reads as a removal and an addition.** The accepted loss. Rewrite a step in wholly different words and Pairing sees no resemblance, so it reports the old one gone and the new one arrived. This is judged the right thing to lose: for a total rewrite, the old text struck through beside the new is more use to a cook than a label reading *Marc rewrote this*.
- **A Ghost's position falls out of the Pairing** rather than being a separate mechanism. [ADR 0014](./0014-a-divergence-is-two-recipes-not-a-difference.md) anchors a Ghost to the line before it in the recipe that really has it and calls the position a best effort; a Pairing produces exactly that, and admits the same uncertainty in the same place.
- **The marking covers the whole recipe, not only the two lists.** Title, Yield, Prep Time, Cook Time and Source are single values — same or not, with nothing to pair and nothing to get wrong. Photographs are exact for free, since [ADR 0017](./0017-a-photograph-is-known-by-its-contents.md) made a Photograph known by its own contents, so *Marc replaced the photo* is a fact rather than a reading. Notes is one block of text compared whole. Section headings are lines and pair like any other.
- **Tags are not marked.** They travel with a recipe and land in the receiving Kitchen's own list, because what one Kitchen means by *quick* is its own business. Marc filing it under "weeknight" and you not is a difference between two filing systems, not between two recipes, and marking it would put a *take his* offer under something nobody would ever want to take.
- **An As Cooked compares against its starting Version by the same Pairing**, so an Attempt's deviations need nothing of their own — which is what [ADR 0005](./0005-an-attempt-is-a-version-you-cooked-but-did-not-keep.md) already assumed when it wrote deviations as ordinary Ingredient Lines and Step text.
- **A Step still points at no Ingredient.** [ADR 0011](./0011-cooking-shows-the-amounts-for-the-step-it-is-on.md) had already settled that the cooking panel works out which Ingredients a Step uses from the Reading, storing nothing and asking nobody to type anything. This decision is the same instinct one layer down, and the glossary's **Step** entry is corrected here to match: a Step carries text and optionally a photograph, and its Ingredients are worked out rather than carried.
- **[#17](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/17) inherits nothing.** The share bundle question was blocked on this one because line ids would have been visible in whatever format it chooses. There are none, so the bundle carries recipes as they are written and no machinery beside them.
- **Pairing is computed on top of what is already stored** ([ADR 0009](./0009-the-v1-cut-line-is-drawn-at-shape-not-at-features.md)), so how well it reads is free to improve after v1 without touching a single stored recipe — the rare case where the cheap answer is also the one that can be made better later.
