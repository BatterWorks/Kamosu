# A recipe is written in a Cookbook and seen in a Kitchen

A recipe belongs to a **Cookbook**, and only that Cookbook's **Co-authors** may change it. Every Person has exactly one Cookbook. It starts with them alone, and two or more people may join theirs into one and later separate again. A **Kitchen** stops holding recipes: it is a group of people, and everyone in it sees and cooks from the Cookbook of every member. Anyone else in the Kitchen who changes your recipe gets a Branch of their own, in their own Cookbook.

This replaces the centre of [ADR 0007](./0007-a-recipe-is-held-by-a-kitchen-not-a-person.md), where one circle, the Kitchen, both held a recipe and decided who saw it. Here there are two circles: the Cookbook decides who changes a recipe, and the Kitchen decides who sees it. Settled on [#125](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/125) on 23 September 2026.

## Why

- **"It's my recipe, and only I should update it."** Under ADR 0007, a Kitchen-mate's tweak became the recipe everyone in the Kitchen cooked from, with nobody asked. That is right for two people writing one cookbook on purpose, and wrong for everyone else: a mother adjusting her son's tartiflette has made *her* tartiflette, not a new official version of his.
- **ADR 0007's objection no longer bites.** It rejected per-person ownership for one reason: it "cannot express co-authoring", so a couple's shared recipes would become a permanent fan of Branches. Joining Cookbooks keeps co-authoring for the people who want it. Everyone else gets a Branch, which is what they wanted.
- **The two circles were separate once and were folded together by accident.** ADR 0007 separated *seeing* (Visibility) from *changing* (the Kitchen). [ADR 0026](./0026-a-recipe-is-seen-by-its-kitchen-or-by-anyone-with-the-link.md) removed *Chosen* on the argument that a Share Link does everything it did, because the reader "takes it into a Kitchen of their own". Two later decisions broke that: Imports always land in the Home Kitchen ([ADR 0025](./0025-an-imported-recipe-is-an-ordinary-recipe.md), [#111](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/111)), and a Copy only starts on a change. For Kitchens you belong to yourself, the link route came back to where it started, and nothing could put your recipe in a second Kitchen of yours. With seeing given to the Kitchen and changing given to the Cookbook, your recipe is the same live recipe in every Kitchen you cook in.
- **"Where does this recipe go?" stops being a question.** It always goes into your Cookbook: what you write, what you import, what arrives in a Bundle and every Branch you start. The **Home Kitchen**, a default that existed only to answer that question, is retired.
- **One Cookbook each, because several was too much to explain.** Several Cookbooks per Person was considered and would have covered every case, at the price of choosing which Cookbook goes into which Kitchen. One each leaves three ideas to learn: your Cookbook, the people you co-write it with, and the Kitchens you cook in.

## Considered options

- **Keep ADR 0007: the Kitchen holds a recipe and every member edits it.** Rejected for the reason above.
- **Keep Kitchens, and change one rule: a recipe belongs to the Person who wrote it.** No new concept at all, and the smallest code change. Rejected because nobody could ever co-write a recipe, and because a recipe still lived in one Kitchen, so [#125](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/125)'s case stayed open.
- **Several Cookbooks per Person, each placed in the Kitchens you choose.** Covers everything, including private experiments kept out of a Kitchen. Rejected as too complicated.
- **One Cookbook per Person, joinable, seen by every Kitchen its Co-authors cook in** (chosen).

ADR 0007 rejected *a Household you belong to exactly one of*, because someone cooks in their own kitchen, a parent's and a supper club's at once. One Cookbook each looks like that option, but it is not. The thing you belong to exactly one of now only decides who may change a recipe. Where you cook stays plural, because Kitchens carry it.

## Consequences

- **A joined Cookbook is seen in every Kitchen either Co-author cooks in.** Nobody confirms each Kitchen when two Cookbooks join: these are recipes, not secrets. Whether joining a Kitchen always brings your Cookbook, or bringing it is a separate choice, is left to the build ticket.
- **A Cookbook leaves a Kitchen once none of its Co-authors cooks there**, whether they left or were removed. Every member who stays keeps a Branch, in their own Cookbook, of each of its recipes they **cooked or branched**. What they branched is already theirs, so in practice this keeps what they cooked. The rest leave with the Cookbook. Their **Attempts** stay readable to them, as ADR 0007 already promised.
- **Separating joined Cookbooks loses nothing.** Each Co-author leaves with a Branch of every recipe in the joined Cookbook, whoever first wrote it. Each Branch carries the full history, and from then on they drift apart like any two Branches.
- **A Kitchen holds no recipes, so there is nothing to strand.** The rule that the last member of a Kitchen may not leave existed so that recipes would not end up in a circle nobody could reach. It goes. A Person cooking alone needs no Kitchen at all, and ADR 0007's "a Person on their own is a Kitchen of one" goes with it.
- **A Copy is starting a Branch in your own Cookbook, and it no longer waits for a change.** It still happens when you change a recipe that is not your Cookbook's. It now also happens when you branch one of your own recipes on purpose, and when a Cookbook leaves a Kitchen and you keep one of its recipes you cooked. The **Copy** entry's "at the moment of the change, never at the moment of receipt" is amended. What stays true is that merely *reading* a recipe never starts one.
- **A Branch can be started on purpose, beside your own, and then it needs a name.** Two versions of a recipe living at once in one Cookbook, "Classic" and "Vegetarian", is a Branch you start deliberately, in your own Cookbook. It asks for a name when you start it, because nothing else tells the two apart. Other Branches are labelled only where a label is needed: a Branch in someone else's Cookbook shows that Cookbook's name, and your own original shows none.
- **You open your own Branch, or else the original.** Tapping a recipe that has several Branches opens the one in your Cookbook if there is one, and otherwise the Branch in the Cookbook that first wrote it. It never opens the most recently changed one, for the reason [ADR 0027](./0027-the-library-is-one-shelf-and-a-result-says-what-matched.md) refused reordering behind your back.
- **[ADR 0014](./0014-a-divergence-is-two-recipes-not-a-difference.md)'s switch must hold more than two Branches.** It assumed exactly two. Under this ADR three or four Branches of one recipe in a family Kitchen is ordinary: yours, your mother's, your brother's, your vegetarian one. How the switch shows them, and what a line marked as different is compared against, is a screen question with options still to be drawn.
- **Your own stays yours once you name it** ([#136](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/136), 27 September 2026). The switch marks every version against your own: your unnamed one where you have one, and otherwise the one of your own you have kept longest, never one sent to you. Naming it changes only the label on its chip, which keeps its name and says *your Cookbook*. So a Branch arriving in your Cookbook, whether a Copy, a joiner's Branch, a Translation or a Bundle coming home, is named after where it came from whenever the Cookbook already holds one of its own of that recipe, named or not. Arriving unnamed, it would quietly become the one the marks compare against. Leaving a Cookbook, the Branch a leaver started loses the joiner's name again, even where the copy beside it already has a name. A way to choose which version the others are compared with, and refusing to name your own, were drawn and not chosen.
- **Nobody writes in a joined Cookbook without having said yes to it** ([#135](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/135), 27 September 2026). Joining moves whole Cookbooks, so accepting an Invite would otherwise bring along everyone the acceptor writes with, and hand the sender's Co-authors new writers too, with none of them asked. Sending the Invite is the sender's yes and accepting it is the acceptor's. Where either Cookbook has anyone else, the join waits until each of them has said yes, on both sides. Who is still asked is worked out afresh each time: someone who leaves meanwhile stops being asked, and the join goes ahead the moment nobody is left. The question is put on Home and on the Cookbook card in Settings. A no from anybody in it calls the join off and reopens the Invite, so the acceptor can leave their own Cookbook, keeping a copy of everything, and open the same link alone. Refusing until the others had left was considered and rejected, because it made the acceptor break up a Cookbook just to be asked. So was letting everyone come along with the Invite page naming them, because the people it named were still never asked. Smaller rules, settled in the build: the acceptor may take their yes back, which reopens the Invite like any other no, while the sender's no ends the Invite, since sending it was their yes. Anybody in the join may call it off until it happens. A Cookbook waits on one join at a time, on either side. An account that is disabled cannot answer and is not asked, and a join is called off once its acceptor can no longer sign in or nobody who can writes the Cookbook it would join.
- **A Cookbook is named after its Co-authors, and any of them may rename it.** "Stéphane's", then "Stéphane & Camille's" once joined. With three or more names that stops being readable, so any Co-author may give it a name of their own. The Cookbook's name labels its Branches on the switch and in the **Thread**.
- **The Hand a Branch records is its Cookbook's, not a Kitchen's.** [ADR 0015](./0015-a-name-is-not-identification-it-is-a-reminder.md) had the Kitchen's Hand label a received Branch ("Chez Dupont"). The Cookbook takes that place. Which Branch a returning Bundle continues is still decided by its Travelling id ([ADR 0020](./0020-a-bundle-is-one-recipes-worth-of-vault.md)), now unique within a Cookbook rather than a Kitchen.
- **The Vault and the import ledger belong to the Cookbook.** Both belonged to the Kitchen because the Kitchen held the recipes. The Vault returns to what [#7](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/7) wanted: "I don't want my vault polluted with their recipes that I didn't try." Whether **Tags** and **Related Recipes** follow the recipes into the Cookbook or stay with the Kitchen as its own filing is left to the build ticket: moving them would stop a Kitchen-mate filing your recipe under a Tag of hers.
- **Nothing is lost moving over.** Every recipe, Version and Attempt carries across, and the existing Kitchens stay as the groups people cook in. Which Cookbook each existing Branch goes to is settled in the build ticket.

## What this amends

- **[ADR 0007](./0007-a-recipe-is-held-by-a-kitchen-not-a-person.md):** a Kitchen no longer holds a recipe or decides who may edit it. The separation of seeing from changing, which that ADR was for, survives and is carried by two circles instead of one. Its Kitchen Name and Nickname, Invites, Access Keys, the Operator and instance-wide Foods are untouched.
- **[ADR 0026](./0026-a-recipe-is-seen-by-its-kitchen-or-by-anyone-with-the-link.md):** a recipe is seen by the Kitchens its Cookbook's Co-authors cook in, or by anyone with its link. Its rejection of *Everyone here* and *Chosen* as per-recipe settings stands.
- **[ADR 0014](./0014-a-divergence-is-two-recipes-not-a-difference.md):** two Branches is no longer the ceiling.
- **[ADR 0015](./0015-a-name-is-not-identification-it-is-a-reminder.md), [ADR 0020](./0020-a-bundle-is-one-recipes-worth-of-vault.md):** the Hand on a Branch and the writer of a Branch are a Cookbook's.
- **[ADR 0025](./0025-an-imported-recipe-is-an-ordinary-recipe.md), [#111](https://github.com/BatterWorks/Kamosu/issues/111):** an Import lands in your Cookbook; the Home Kitchen is retired, and whether an Import should let you choose where it goes no longer arises.
- **[ADR 0027](./0027-the-library-is-one-shelf-and-a-result-says-what-matched.md):** the shelf is your Cookbook and every Cookbook in a Kitchen you cook in. Its rejection of *recipes you personally wrote* as a filter "because it splits a shared Kitchen into his and hers" argued from the rule this ADR replaces; the *My recipes* filter itself is unchanged.
- **[ADR 0035](./0035-a-tag-is-how-a-kitchen-files-not-what-a-recipe-is.md):** whether a Tag belongs to a Cookbook or a Kitchen is reopened; its argument holds either way.
- **[ADR 0005](./0005-an-attempt-is-a-version-you-cooked-but-did-not-keep.md):** an Attempt inherits who can see its recipe, and a recipe is now seen in Kitchens its cook may never have joined. Who sees an Attempt is reopened in the build ticket.
- **[ADR 0040](./0040-a-refusal-answers-as-if-the-thing-were-not-here.md):** its rule now governs two checks, *may change* (Co-author) and *may see* (Kitchen), instead of Kitchen membership.
