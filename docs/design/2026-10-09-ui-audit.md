# The interface, looked at on three sizes — 9 October 2026

**This is a dated record, and it is never updated.** It says what was seen on
one day. Anything it names may have moved since; the code is what is current.

## How it was looked at

Every screen was opened on the dev instance in WebKit, the engine Safari uses,
at a phone (390 × 844), an upright tablet (834 × 1194), a laptop (1440 × 900)
and a phone turned sideways (844 × 390). The main flows were driven as well:
search, the + menu, the sheets, the editor, a whole cooking, and the shopping
list with three recipes on it. The test cooking and the shopping items were
deleted afterwards.

The identity holds across all three sizes: the rail, the two-column recipe,
Foods beside what is open and the cooking screen at distance read as one app.
What follows is what to change, most important first. The numbers are the
ones the prototype folder (`.dev/prototypes/218-ui-audit/`) and the tickets
filed from this record refer to: #218 (the phone's navigation), #219 (the way
back), #220 (Cook this and the acts), #221 (Settings), #222 (the phone
sideways), #223 (shelves and tags), #224 (dates, titles, words), #225 (small
controls), #226 (layout fixes), #227 (the wide diary), #228 (three questions).
The choices made on the prototype on 9 October 2026 are comments on those
tickets.

## Broken

1. **Phone sideways, cooking screen: the Step disappears.** At 844 × 390 the
   amounts, the control row and Back/Next fill the window and the Step gets no
   lines at all. The Room rule keeps a short window on the phone layout on
   purpose (ADR 0044), but the phone layout has no answer for a short window.
   The recipe page sideways is nearly as bad: header, notice card and tab bar
   leave about 80px of content.
2. **Shopping on the phone: a recipe's title is squeezed to about 70px**, so
   "A really big chicken parm" wraps to four lines. "shopping for 4 servings"
   and "TAKE OFF THE LIST" share its row.
3. **Dates are written five ways, and some ignore the chosen language.** One
   visit showed "25/09/2026", "September 28", "October 7, 2026 at 01:58 AM"
   and "09/10/2026, 20:51:45". Some `toLocaleDateString` calls pass the app's
   language and the rest take the browser's, so a French interface can show
   English dates. Session times carry seconds.
4. **No page sets `<title>`.** A tab on a Mac or an iPad shows the address,
   history is unreadable and a bookmark says nothing.
5. **Cooking screen on the phone, step 4: the "4 servings" chip truncates to
   "4…"** once "Set a timer for 28 min" stands beside it.
6. **Plurals:** "1 recipes are on your shelf" and "Show all 1" on an import's
   page.
7. **Red on a note that is no warning.** On a Food's page "A Food needs at
   least one name, so the last one stays" is set in beni though nothing is
   wrong.
8. **A cooking opened in the wide diary** draws its body as a narrower box that
   starts below the card's edge, so it reads as clipped. To be checked on the
   real tablet.

## Copy that names the wrong device

9. "Your library isn't on this **phone** yet", "Keep Kamosu on your **phone**"
   and the Settings heading "This phone" all appear on a tablet and a laptop.
10. The install card is shown on the sign-in page before anyone is signed in,
    and on a laptop where it means nothing. The two notice cards arrive one
    after the other: put the library card away and the install card takes its
    place, pushing the page down 230px again. #214 covers the sign-in page's
    look on the wide layout; this is a separate point.

## Getting around

11. **"Cook this" sits at the foot of a 4,200px page on the phone**, five
    screens past every ingredient and Step.
12. **Seven stacked full-width buttons close the recipe page** (Edit, Cook,
    History, Language, Share, Print, Shopping list). On a laptop they are 630
    wide and 60 tall each.
13. **The phone has no consistent way back.** Imports, Crouton and Thread use a
    small "‹ Settings" text link; the recipe page has none. The wide layout has
    a round back arrow.
14. **Settings is one page 8,000px tall on a phone, with fourteen sections.**
15. **Signed out on a phone, the header's You button and the four tabs are
    still drawn**, and lead nowhere useful. The wide layout already hides the
    rail for a stranger.
16. **Sideways scrolling on a mouse.** On a laptop and a tablet the Home
    shelves still scroll sideways with the eighth tile cut off, and the tag
    chips on Recipes are cut at "South American" with the scrollbar hidden.
17. **Header and tab bar take 19% of a phone screen on every page**, the long
    recipe page included, where the header holds nothing the reader needs.

## Touch targets and small type

18. **Settings: Sign out, End, Rename, Save, Keep it, Change your name** are
    12.5px underlined words, some 20px apart.
19. **The cooking screen's top row on the phone:** "Pause · 1/4 · Screen may
    sleep · Not really cooking" at label size. Two are tappable, two are
    statements, and they look alike.
20. **"Add something of your own" and "Add a recipe" on Shopping** are plain
    small text with no affordance; they read as captions.
21. **The 10.5px uppercase label** (TAGS, INGREDIENTS, TAKE OFF THE LIST, the
    tab labels) is at the edge of legibility on a phone.

## Keyboard and accessibility

22. **Keyboard focus is the browser's default blue ring**, which clashes with
    indigo on paper and is barely visible on a chip. `app.css` has no
    `:focus-visible` rule.
23. **No dark mode.** Likely deliberate, and the cooking screen is already
    dark; an ADR saying so would settle it.
24. **Type is in px**, so a phone's own text-size setting does nothing.

## Wide layout polish

25. **"Before you start" on a laptop:** − and + stand 1,500px apart with
    "4 servings" floating between them.
26. **Notice cards span the full 1,330px on a laptop** with two enormous
    buttons.
27. **The editor draws Recipe and Remove under every ingredient line:** twenty
    lines, forty buttons.
28. **Three looks for one kind of act.** The same action is sometimes a filled
    button, sometimes outlined, sometimes an underlined word ("Save", "Keep
    it", "Delete this recipe", "Read recipes in another language").
29. **Cover shapes repeat visibly** in a 90-recipe library: three green
    croissants on one shelf. Eight shapes across the dyes.

## Asked for alongside

- The phone's tab bar to take the rail's look, so the three sizes share one
  navigation.
- The header's You card to become a plain Settings control, as the rail's is.
