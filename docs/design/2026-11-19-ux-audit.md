# The everyday tasks, walked in pictures by three evaluators, 19 November 2026

**This is a dated record, and it is never updated.** It says what was measured,
walked and rated on one day. Anything it names may have moved since; the code
is what is current. The measures and their thresholds are those of
`docs/research/ux-reading-crowding-and-finding.md`, section 3, and the research
it names is that note's section 1.

## How it was done

**Measured.** `just ux-measure` on its full coverage: 55 screens of a copy of a
working library and of an empty instance, 444 rows across the two comfort sizes
(402 × 874 and 412 × 924), the 360 px breakage pass, the upright iPad and the
laptop, in the four Languages. It ran on throwaway instances and left the dev
database unchanged. Its table is `2026-11-19-ux-audit-measures.csv`, beside
this record. The pictures its last column names are not in the repository.

**Walked.** `just ux-walk` recorded the shortest path of twelve tasks on a fresh
copy of the Sample Library at 402 × 874 in English: 48 pictures, one before every
tap and one at each finish line. The tasks: find a recipe by searching, by
browsing and by tag; read it; share it; cook from it; put it on the shopping
list; plan it for tomorrow; add one from a link; and, as occasional tasks,
change the language, invite someone to a Kitchen and find what a recipe said
before its last change. The map this audit belongs to (#627) adds no task.

**Three evaluators.** GPT-6.2-sol in Codex, Fable 5.2 and Opus 5.5 in Claude
Code, each at high effort. Each walked all twelve tasks in an order of its own
(declared order, reverse, and a fixed shuffle starting at share), taking one
picture at a time from the hand-over command, writing what it would tap, why,
and what was unclear before seeing the next, and noting every problem against
Nielsen's ten heuristics as it met it. None saw another's notes.

Each ran in a sealed box: its own empty home folder holding only the agent's
settings and the hand-over folder, no copy of the repository, no instruction
files, skills or memory of the maintainer's, and no network but a proxy that let
the two model services through and logged every request. Web search and helper
agents were switched off in each agent, since a hosted search runs on the
provider's side, past the proxy; Codex's plug-ins, apps and browser use too.
Claude Code still listed its own built-in skills, and called none. The first
lines of each transcript show nothing loaded beyond the brief. Both Claude
transcripts show every picture opened after the command handed it over; Codex's
log does not record when a picture is viewed. GPT-6.2-sol started first, as the
pilot: once its first task had gone through cleanly, the other two started
beside it.

**Merged and rated.** The three evaluators wrote 227 notes (69, 96 and 62). They
merge into 99 distinct problems, 52 of them found by all three. Each of the three
models then rated every merged problem from 0 to 4 in a fresh session of its
own, with the pictures, and the severity below is the mean of the three
([Nielsen's severity
ratings](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/how-to-rate-the-severity-of-usability-problems/)).
98 were rated in one session per model. The 99th, the invite link's address
(I9), was first set aside as a quirk of the test copy; the review of this record
found that the invite link takes the address in the inviter's browser while the
share link takes the instance's public address, so it was put back and rated in
a second fresh session per model. One note is left out: the share link's
address ends in .example because the Sample Library's public address does, by
design. The three gave the same rating to 48 of the 99, and differed by two
points on one. Their ratings correlate at 0.61 to 0.70. GPT-6.2-sol rated
highest (mean 1.88), Fable 5.2 lowest (1.48).

The Claude sessions cost 9.57 dollars as Claude Code reports it; Codex reports
tokens, not a price.

**What the method cannot see.** How often people meet each problem is a guess,
since Kamosu keeps no usage data: the raters judged it from the task. The
evaluators saw still pictures of the recorded path, so a problem off that path,
or one a hand would feel and an eye would not, is missing. Every picture is
English at one phone size. All three evaluators are models, and two share a
maker. Decision 8 of the map puts people in front of the screens after the first
fixes.

## What stands out

**The recipe page hides what people come to do.** Share, the shopping list and
History sit in a grid at the foot of the page, after the whole method, and the
first screen shows none of the recipe itself. Three of the twelve tasks needed a
control from that grid (share, shopping and old version), and on each all
three evaluators had to scroll and hunt. Every rater put it at 3, and it
explains several lower problems.

**Sharing is the worst single moment.** The screen says in its smallest text
that a share carries every version, name and note, offers a link and a zip file
with no word on which a friend can read, turns the link on in one tap, and
then only copies it. GPT-6.2-sol rated it a catastrophe.

**Kamosu's own words reach the screen everywhere.** Kitchen, Cookbook,
Household, Version, shelf, Operator, agent, "let go", "ways in": each evaluator,
starting with no glossary, stopped on them. 25 problems break "match between system and the real
world" first, and 17 of those average under 2: minor one by one, many
together.

**The measures agree with the walk.** The header and tab bar take 16.9% of the
first screenful on every screen that has both (48 of 55; limit 15). 49 of 55 screens carry
more than 20 interface words on their first screenful. The share screen holds
six sentences of explanation where the threshold allows none.

**Almost nothing failed outright.** The evaluators' guesses followed the
recorded path, or another route that also works, at almost every step. On the
read task all three searched where the path scrolls, and on the browse task
Opus 5.5 used a tag. A gear guessed for Settings or a + for Add a recipe counts
as a match. The one wrong turn: at the end of the old-version task Opus 5.5
decided the version it was shown was not the one wanted, because the screen
says every line is as now and nothing says the last save changed only the
photo (V8). The rest are hesitation, guessing and missed controls, which is why
only five average 2.5 or more.

## Measures over their thresholds

At 402 × 874 in English, from `just ux-measure`. 412 × 924 agrees, a little
roomier.

- **Room**: 16.9% on the 48 screens with a header and tab bar (limit 15). The
  cooking screen and the pages a stranger opens are under it.
- **Words**: 49 of 55 screens over 20 interface words on the first screenful.
  The heaviest everyday ones: the plan (77), a Food (115), the + menu (106), the
  share screen (81), Settings (83). The agent page in Settings holds 4,879 on
  one page, 38.8 screenfuls long. French, Spanish and Dutch run a few words over
  English; Dutch adds one sentence over 25 words on the plan.
- **Sentences of explanation**: the share screen holds 6 on its first screenful
  where the threshold allows none; Home and Recipes one each, also against a
  limit of none.
- **Controls and main buttons**: Recipes and search hold 18 controls (limit 16)
  and 5 main buttons (limit 2); the main buttons are recipe tiles, which the
  measure counts as filled. The + menu open holds 25.
- **Tap size**: 26 screens have a control under 48 px. The everyday ones:
  Recipes' filter chips (33 px tall), a long recipe's tag chips and source line
  (26 controls), the plan's add buttons (40 × 33), the cooking log on Cooked.
  Settings' tag list holds 178.
- **Gaps**: the + menu, the Kitchens page, a recipe's history and the About page
  put controls closer than 8 px, and the Kitchens page has one pair of overlapping tapped
  areas.
- **Length**: Settings 2.2 screenfuls, upkeep 3.0, the people page 2.2 (limit 2
  for a screen that is not a list). Recipes, a list, is 27.4, which the limit
  does not cover. Every main action starts within the first screenful.
- **Breakage at 360 px**: the header's "Kamosu" is cut off on 51 of 55 screens.
  Recipe titles are cut on Recipes, as their design means. The cooking setup
  draws "Before you start" over "How much are you making?". The overlaps counted
  on the + menu (a menu over the list) and on the About tour (its drawing of a
  phone) are by design; the 42 on the agent page in Settings were not looked
  at.
- **Type and colour**: almost every screen shows four or more font sizes and
  five or more text colours (a flag, never a failure).

## The problems, most severe first

Each line gives the three ratings and their mean, how many evaluators found it,
the pictures (task and number, as `just ux-walk` writes them), the heuristic, the
measure it breaks where one does, and the research behind it: a section of the
research note's part 1. A problem marked as asking
for something new is outside this audit by the map's decision 4.

### Major: average 2.5 and over

1. **Sharing carries every version, name and note, said in the smallest text, and turns on in one tap** (S3, Share). The warning that private notes and names go with the recipe is the palest text on the screen. There is no way to share only the current recipe, no preview of what goes, and no confirm step.
   Severity 3.33 (GPT-6.2-sol 4, Fable 5.2 3, Opus 5.5 3); found by 3 of 3; pictures share 3, share 4. Heuristic: Error prevention; User control and freedom.
   Words: the share screen holds 6 sentences of explanation on its first screenful (limit 0), 81 interface words (limit 20).
   Research: Banner blindness; How little people read.

2. **Share, shopping list, History, Language, Print and Edit are only at the very bottom of the recipe** (P2, Recipe). They appear after the whole method, the note, related recipes, the cooking log and photos. Nothing at the top hints that they exist; three of the twelve tasks needed one.
   Severity 3.00 (GPT-6.2-sol 3, Fable 5.2 3, Opus 5.5 3); found by 3 of 3; pictures share 1, share 2, shopping 1, shopping 2, old-version 1, old-version 2. Heuristic: Recognition rather than recall; Flexibility and efficiency of use.
   Depth: Share this recipe, Add to shopping list and History each need a scroll past the whole recipe (`depth.csv`).
   Research: Hidden navigation; Progressive disclosure.

3. **The long list has no stated order, no sort and no jump, and search scrolls away** (R7, Recipes). 40 recipes as tall cards, about four per screen, apparently A to Z but never said. Reaching S means passing about thirty cards; the search box and filters are gone once scrolled, with no way back to the top.
   Severity 3.00 (GPT-6.2-sol 3, Fable 5.2 3, Opus 5.5 3); found by 3 of 3; pictures find-browse 2, find-browse 3, read 3. Heuristic: Flexibility and efficiency of use; Visibility of system status.
   Recipes is 27.4 screenfuls long at 402 × 874, a list, so no length limit applies; the walk found the cost.
   Research: Progressive disclosure.

4. **The cooking screen has no visible way out and does not name the recipe** (C1, Cooking). Full screen, no tab bar or title. Back is greyed; Pause and an unlabelled ⋯ are the only top controls. Someone who came in by mistake has to guess.
   Severity 2.67 (GPT-6.2-sol 3, Fable 5.2 3, Opus 5.5 2); found by 3 of 3; pictures cook 2, cook 3. Heuristic: User control and freedom; Visibility of system status.
   Research: Apple's Human Interface Guidelines (a way out of every view).

5. **An invite link carries the address the inviter happens to have the app open at** (I9, Invite). The Kitchen and Cookbook invite links are built from the address in the inviter's browser (on the test copy, "http://localhost:…"), while a recipe's share link uses the instance's public address. Someone who opens the app at a home-network address sends a friend outside the home a link that cannot open, and neither screen says which address a link carries.
   Severity 2.67 (GPT-6.2-sol 3, Fable 5.2 2, Opus 5.5 3); found by 2 of 3; pictures invite 4, share 4. Heuristic: Error prevention; Help users recognize, diagnose and recover from errors; Consistency and standards.
   Research: Cognitive load, heuristics and evaluation (error prevention).
