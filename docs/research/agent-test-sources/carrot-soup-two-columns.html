Spiced carrot and red lentil soup

Autumn carrots are sweet enough that this soup needs nothing more than a little spice and a squeeze of lemon at the end. The lentils thicken it without any cream, and it reheats well, so make the whole pot and eat it over two or three days.

Toasting the cumin seeds in a dry pan takes a minute and gives the soup most of its flavour, so don't skip it. If you have no seeds, use one teaspoon of ground cumin and add it with the chilli.

The soup is thick enough to stand a spoon in when it has just been blended. Thin it with a little more stock or water if you like it looser, and taste for salt again after you do.

Serve it with warm flatbread, a spoonful of plain yogurt and a few coriander leaves. Children who will not eat carrots have been known to eat this.

Serves 4. Takes 40 minutes.

Ingredients

  • 2 tsp cumin seeds
  • a pinch of chilli flakes
  • 2 tbsp olive oil
  • 600 g carrots, coarsely grated
  • 140 g split red lentils
  • 1 litre hot vegetable stock
  • 125 ml milk
  • juice of ½ lemon
  • plain yogurt and coriander, to serve

Method

  1. Toast the cumin seeds and chilli flakes in a large dry pan for 1 minute, until they start to jump. Tip half of them out and keep for later.
  2. Add the oil, carrots, lentils, stock and milk to the pan and bring to the boil. Simmer for 15 minutes, until the lentils have swollen and softened.
  3. Blend the soup until smooth, then stir in the lemon juice and season with salt.
  4. Serve with a spoonful of yogurt, the reserved spices and a few coriander leaves.
Freezes well for up to 3 months. Thaw overnight in the fridge and reheat gently.
